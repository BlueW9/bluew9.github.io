Understanding the abuse

Honour-based abuse

Honour-based abuse is violence, threats, intimidation, coercion or control used to punish or prevent behaviour that a family or community sees as bringing “shame”. It can be physical, sexual, psychological, emotional or financial, and it often involves several family members or the wider community acting together. It can include forced marriage, imprisonment at home, threats to kill and murder.

Forced marriage

A forced marriage is one where at least one person does not consent and pressure or abuse is used: threats, violence, emotional blackmail or fear of bringing shame on the family. It is different from an arranged marriage, where both people freely agree to the match and can say no.

Warning signs

  • Being stopped from attending school, work or appointments, or being kept at home.
  • Phone, internet use and movements being monitored or restricted.
  • Passport, ID or birth certificate taken away.
  • Sudden pressure to travel abroad, often described as a holiday or a family visit.
  • Threats or punishment over clothing, friendships, relationships or opinions.
  • A sudden change in a young woman's behaviour: withdrawing, absences, self-harm or fear of going home.

Shame belongs to the person who commits the abuse. Seeking safety is never a betrayal and never a crime.

If you are at risk: safety planning

Every situation is different, and a specialist can help you build a plan around yours. These steps are commonly recommended by specialist services.

Tell someone you trust

Choose one person who will not pass information to your family: a teacher, a doctor, a friend outside the community, or a specialist helpline. Agree a code word that means “I need help now”.

Prepare your documents

If it is safe, photograph or copy your passport, ID, birth certificate, visa or immigration papers, and bank details. Keep copies with the trusted person or in a secure online account that your family cannot access. Keep originals where you can reach them quickly.

Protect your digital footprint

  • Use a device your family cannot access, such as a library computer or a trusted friend's phone. Private browsing hides history on a device but does not stop spyware or a shared account.
  • Turn off location sharing and check which apps and family accounts can see where you are.
  • Create a new email address that your family does not know, and change important passwords from a safe device.
  • Save helpline numbers under a name that will not raise questions.

Before any trip abroad

Forced marriages are often carried out overseas during a family trip. If you are worried, speak to a specialist service before you travel. Memorise or hide the number of a helpline and of your country's embassy or consulate, and tell your trusted person your dates and destination. Where possible, avoid the trip.

If you decide to leave

Contact a specialist service first. They can help you plan the safest way and time to leave, arrange emergency accommodation, and explain your legal options. Take your documents, phone, charger, any medication and some money if you can.

Get help now

If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number: 999 (UK), 112 (Germany and the EU), 911 (US and Canada).

United Kingdom

Karma Nirvana: Honour Based Abuse Helpline

Confidential support for victims and survivors of honour-based abuse and forced marriage. Phone 0800 5999 247, Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. karmanirvana.org.uk

Forced Marriage Unit (UK Government)

Advice for anyone facing forced marriage, in the UK or overseas. Phone +44 20 7008 0151, Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm. Out of hours: +44 20 7008 1500 (ask for the Global Response Centre). gov.uk/stop-forced-marriage

National Domestic Abuse Helpline

Free, confidential, 24 hours a day, with translation services. Phone 0808 2000 247.

Germany

Hilfetelefon “Gewalt gegen Frauen”

National helpline run by the Federal Ministry for Family Affairs: free, anonymous, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, in 18 languages. Phone 116 016 (also 08000 116 016). Online chat and email at hilfetelefon.de

Terre des Femmes: Zwangsheirat

A directory of specialist counselling centres against forced marriage across Germany: zwangsheirat.de

United States

National Domestic Violence Hotline

24 hours a day. Phone 1-800-799-7233.

Tahirih Justice Center: Forced Marriage Initiative

Legal and social support for people facing or fleeing forced marriage, including overseas. Email FMI@tahirih.org. This is not a 24-hour line. preventforcedmarriage.org

If you are helping someone

Do

  • Speak to her alone, in private, and believe what she tells you.
  • Ask what she needs and let her make the decisions.
  • Keep what she tells you confidential, especially from family and community.
  • Use a professional interpreter if one is needed.
  • Contact a specialist helpline for guidance and share the information with her.
  • Record what she says, with dates, in a secure place.

Do not

  • Contact her family or community leaders, or try to mediate. This can put her in serious danger.
  • Use relatives or friends as interpreters.
  • Send her away because it seems like a “family matter”.
  • Pressure her to leave, to stay or to press charges.
  • Share her information without her consent unless her life is at risk.
